<StageMeta type="FitBoxesToInk" />

Every source of boxes reports them a little short of the glyphs. A text detector
misses what is thin and isolated at a line's ends, such as the looping capital
of handwriting, because those strokes score low on its probability map. A PDF
text layer sizes boxes from font metrics, which a script font's flourishes and
descenders overrun. For reading, a clipped capital turns `Freya` into `reya`. For
redaction, a box short of its text leaves the edge of a name showing.

This stage checks each box against the pixels and grows it until the ink it
belongs to is inside. Boxes only ever grow, and their text is untouched.

```ts
import { Pipeline, FitBoxesToInk } from '@stabrise/scaledp'
import { PdfToImage } from '@stabrise/scaledp/pdf'
import { DbnetOnnxDetector, PaddleRecognizer } from '@stabrise/scaledp/ocr'

const rows = await new Pipeline([
    new PdfToImage({ resolution: 200 }),
    new DbnetOnnxDetector({ outputCol: 'detected' }),
    // Before reading, so the crop contains the whole word.
    new FitBoxesToInk({ inputCols: ['image', 'detected'], outputCol: 'detected' }),
    new PaddleRecognizer({ inputCols: ['image', 'detected'] }),
    // After reading, so the final boxes cover the glyphs.
    new FitBoxesToInk({ inputCols: ['image', 'text'], outputCol: 'text' }),
]).transform(file)
```

It reads `bboxes` off a `DetectorOutput` or a `Document` and writes the same
value back with the boxes grown, by default in place. The boxes must be in the
image's own pixels: the embedded picture a detector ran on, or the page after
`PdfMergeImageText`.

## How a box is fitted

**Ink is told from background locally.** The background is the median
brightness of a thin ring just outside the box, where it is the great majority
even when a stroke escapes. Ink is anything that differs from it by at least
half the contrast the box's own text has. So dark text on paper, white text on
black and text on a coloured highlight all work, and a low-contrast box is left
alone rather than guessed at.

**Each edge steps outwards** one pixel line at a time while the line just past it
still holds ink:

| Direction | Crosses a blank gap of | Stops at |
| --- | --- | --- |
| along the text | up to `gapRatio` × height (0.15; a word space is ~0.25) | the end of the ink, or `maxGrowAlong` × height |
| across the text | nothing | the first blank row, or `maxGrowAcross` × height |

Crossing small gaps along the line is what reaches a capital written a little
apart from the rest of its word. Never crossing one across the line is what keeps
two lines from merging. Two passes are made, so a swash leaving the box at a
corner is reached along the text and then its top across it.

**A box never grows into another box.** Each edge may move at most to the nearest
box beyond it: along the line for left and right, among boxes overlapping it for
top and bottom. In a PDF text layer each word's box runs almost to the next
word's start, a gap of a few pixels, well inside the letter-gap tolerance, so ink
alone would merge every word with the next. A box counts as beyond an edge only
when its centre is, so an invisible text layer's box stacked on the same word
doesn't pin the OCR box in place.

**Thin rules don't count.** A column needs a tenth of the line's height in ink to
carry a box along, so an underline or a table rule running out of the box, one
or two pixels thick, cannot drag it sideways.

**Rotated boxes** are fitted in their own frame, along and across their line, and
keep their angle.

## Parameters

| Parameter | Type | Default | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `inputCols` | `string[]` (2) | `['image', 'boxes']` | The image the boxes are in, then the boxes: a detector output or a Document. |
| `maxGrowAlong` | `number` 0–5 | `0.75` | Most a box may grow at each end of its line, as a fraction of its height. |
| `maxGrowAcross` | `number` 0–5 | `0.5` | Most a box may grow above and below, as a fraction of its height. It stops at the first blank row anyway, so lines never merge. |
| `gapRatio` | `number` 0–1 | `0.15` | Blank gap along the text still crossed, as a fraction of height -- reaches a capital written apart from its word. A word space is about 0.25. |
